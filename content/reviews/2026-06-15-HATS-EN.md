## One-sentence summary

HATS uses a human-pilot plus MLLM-copilot paradigm for four-arm teleoperation: a human directly controls two primary arms, while an agent controls two assistive arms under real-time voice supervision.

![HATS overview](/reviews/2026-06-15-HATS-EN/fig1-overview.png)

*Figure 1. HATS enables one operator, assisted by an MLLM-based copilot, to collect four-arm manipulation data.*

## Problem addressed

Many real-world manipulation tasks exceed the capability of bimanual systems: lifting wide objects, spreading cloth, and coordinating actions across large workspaces often require more than two arms. Multi-arm systems can improve efficiency, but demonstration collection is difficult. A single operator faces high cognitive load and mode-switching overhead, while multiple operators create communication and coordination costs.

HATS aims for a middle ground: **one human operator assisted by an MLLM copilot that controls the auxiliary arms.**

## Methodology

![System overview](/reviews/2026-06-15-HATS-EN/fig2-system-overview.png)

*Figure 2. HATS consists of visual perception, LLM task planning, human-agent collaboration, and voice-based supervision.*

The four arms are divided functionally:

- **Primary arms** are directly teleoperated by the human;
- **Assistive arms** are controlled by a training-free MLLM-based agent that performs support subtasks.

The system has four modules:

1. **Visual perception**, using AnyGrasp and VLM outputs to obtain grasp poses and target positions;
2. **Task planning**, where an LLM planner decomposes the task into executable actions for the assistive arms;
3. **Human-agent collaboration**, where human-controlled and agent-controlled arms execute in parallel;
4. **Human-in-the-loop supervision**, where voice commands trigger retry, stop, go-home, and other high-priority corrections.

This division keeps the human in charge of high-value manipulation while offloading structured support behaviors to the agent.

## Experiments and results

![Task suite](/reviews/2026-06-15-HATS-EN/fig3-task-suite.png)

*Figure 3. The evaluation includes seven real multi-arm tasks: Clear, Pack, Draw, Clean, Pour, Spread, and Lift.*

The seven tasks are grouped by coupling strength: independent execution, weakly coupled coordination, and strongly coupled coordination. HATS is compared against Solo-Switching, Full-Agent, and Dual-Human baselines.

![System results](/reviews/2026-06-15-HATS-EN/fig4-system-results.png)

*Figure 4. HATS is competitive in system-level task success and produces downstream policy data comparable to dual-human demonstrations.*

The main results are:

- HATS reaches **86.4% average success**, close to Dual-Human at 90.0%;
- it reduces the concurrency bottleneck in strongly coupled tasks;
- downstream imitation policies trained on HATS data perform comparably to those trained on Dual-Human data;
- the user study shows lower NASA-TLX workload.

![Workload](/reviews/2026-06-15-HATS-EN/fig5-workload.png)

*Figure 5. Subjective workload results show reduced physical and temporal demand under HATS.*

## Hardware and planning details

![Hardware setup](/reviews/2026-06-15-HATS-EN/fig6-hardware-setup.png)

*Figure 6. The hardware uses four Agilex Piper arms, RealSense cameras, and a GELLO-style leader control interface.*

![Planner prompt](/reviews/2026-06-15-HATS-EN/fig7-planner-prompt.png)

*Figure 7. The LLM planner uses a structured system prompt that enforces spatial constraints, collision avoidance, and JSON output format.*

The agent is not an end-to-end low-level controller. It generates structured motion-primitive plans through prompting, and a scheduler executes them with validation. This reduces openness, but improves stability, predictability, and debuggability.

## Strengths

- **Practical problem framing** around multi-arm demonstration collection;
- clear human-agent division of labor;
- evaluation includes success, timing, downstream policy quality, and workload;
- voice supervision provides a low-cost correction and safety channel;
- strong relevance to scalable robot-data collection.

## Weaknesses

- the agent relies on predefined motion primitives rather than open-ended action generation;
- tasks are structured, so adaptation to highly dynamic scenes remains limited;
- the operator must still monitor the assistive arms;
- four-arm hardware is expensive and difficult to reproduce;
- LLM reliability depends on prompts, validators, and task structure.

## Discussion questions

1. How should control authority shift dynamically between human and agent in multi-arm teleoperation?
2. Is voice supervision fast enough for contact-rich failures, or should visual/tactile anomaly detection be added?
3. Does HATS data inherit biases from the agent planner?
4. Can the copilot learn online from human corrections?
5. Does the pilot-copilot paradigm scale beyond four arms?

## Relevance to our lab

HATS is highly relevant to teleoperation-based data collection and shared autonomy. Its key lesson is that demonstration systems need not choose between all-human and all-autonomous control. A structured human-agent division can reduce labor while preserving high-quality demonstrations.

## Possible follow-up ideas

- introduce a HATS-style copilot into bimanual or dexterous teleoperation;
- use visual or tactile anomaly detectors to trigger safety interventions beyond voice;
- study planner-induced bias in collected demonstrations;
- let the copilot learn task-specific coordination from human corrections;
- replace fixed motion primitives with VLA or world-model-based action modules.
