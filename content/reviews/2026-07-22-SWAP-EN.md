## One-sentence summary

SWAP writes quadrupedal left-right physical symmetry directly into the world model and controller, improving bilateral coordination for a 2.13 m gap leap and a 1.63 m platform climb.

## Problem addressed

Standard latent world models learn left-right mirrored physical interactions as independent patterns, wasting representation capacity. In extreme parkour, where successful trajectories occupy a very small region of the action space, this redundancy increases exploration difficulty and encourages asymmetric local optima that overuse one side of the robot.

The paper asks how reflection symmetry can be imposed as a strict structural prior across perception, prediction, and motor control.

## Main contribution

- An end-to-end symmetric equivariant world model and actor-critic architecture.
- Consistent reflection equivariance in encoders, RSSM dynamics, decoders, and control networks.
- Zero-shot transfer from unilaterally trained terrain to unseen mirrored terrain.
- Large-scale gap leaping and platform climbing on the Apollo quadruped.

## Methodology

![SWAP architecture combining a symmetry-equivariant world model and actor-critic.](/reviews/2026-07-22-SWAP-EN/fig1-framework.png)

*Figure 1. SWAP architecture combining a symmetry-equivariant world model and actor-critic.*

SWAP builds on a recurrent state-space model. Symmetric equivariant CNNs encode depth images, while equivariant MLP and recurrent modules process proprioception, prior/posterior distributions, and latent dynamics. Features are organized in left-right pairs, and reflection is implemented through channel permutation so mirrored inputs produce mirrored latent states and reconstructions.

The controller uses an equivariant actor and an invariant critic: mirrored observations generate mirrored actions, while value estimates remain unchanged. The world model updates at 10 Hz and control runs at 50 Hz. End-to-end training uses 6,000 Isaac Gym environments for about 10 hours on one RTX 4090, with adaptive curricula for boxes, gaps, stairs, slopes, and rocks.

## Experimental setup

![Gap leaping, box climbing, failure modes, and sim-to-real motion sequences.](/reviews/2026-07-22-SWAP-EN/fig3-parkour-results.png)

*Figure 2. Gap leaping, box climbing, failure modes, and sim-to-real motion sequences.*

Simulation compares full SWAP, equivariant world model only, no equivariance, and a soft symmetry loss. Mirrored generalization trains exclusively on left-high/right-low terrain and tests directly on horizontal mirrors. Each difficulty point contains 1,500 trials.

Hardware uses a 72 kg Apollo quadruped. Indoor tests measure extreme gap and box performance; outdoor zero-shot tests include wet granite, water reflections, dark stairs, grass, cardboard, rubble, and movable carpets.

## Key results

![Zero-shot transfer success rates and image reconstruction on mirrored terrains.](/reviews/2026-07-22-SWAP-EN/fig2-symmetry-results.png)

*Figure 3. Zero-shot transfer success rates and image reconstruction on mirrored terrains.*

On unseen mirrored box terrain, SWAP's depth reconstruction error is `4.68 ± 6.49 cm`, compared with `9.32 ± 12.50 cm` without equivariance. The full architecture outperforms world-model-only equivariance, soft symmetry, and unconstrained variants in box climbing while maintaining stable bilateral contacts.

The real Apollo crosses a `2.13 m` gap and climbs a `1.63 m` platform, approximately three times its body length and standing hip height. One policy transfers without environment-specific tuning across diverse perceptual and contact disturbances.

## Strengths

- Physical symmetry is embedded as a hard architecture rather than data augmentation alone.
- Constraining both the model and policy prevents representational and behavioral inconsistency.
- Mirrored out-of-distribution tests directly evaluate structural generalization.
- Hardware parkour and outdoor generalization are compelling.

## Weaknesses

- Record comparisons span robots with different size, mass, and hardware.
- The design focuses on bilateral reflection and does not study other symmetry groups.
- Depth perception remains necessary.
- The architecture is more complex than a standard world model.

## Discussion questions

1. Can hard equivariance limit adaptation under asymmetric damage or payload?
2. How can symmetry breaking be learned while retaining a structural prior?
3. Can equivariant world models support dual-arm manipulation or humanoid control?
4. How should extreme performance be normalized fairly across robots?

## Relevance to our lab

The paper is relevant to Physical AI world models, reinforcement-learning locomotion, and high-performance embodiment control. It demonstrates how analyzable physical structure can be embedded in learning systems, aligning with a model-grounded cerebellum and learning-based brain architecture.

## Possible follow-up ideas

- Develop flexible equivariance for payload, damage, and asymmetric embodiment.
- Extend to rotational, permutation, and multi-robot symmetries.
- Combine the world model with long-horizon task planning.
- Evaluate on humanoid parkour and dual-arm manipulation.
