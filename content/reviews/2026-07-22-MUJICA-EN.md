## One-sentence summary

MUJICA allows Go2-W to select among locomotion, high-platform climbing, and fall recovery without vision or manual switching, while keeping actions inside the true motor-feasible region.

## Problem addressed

Blind locomotion policies usually cover closely related skills, while dynamically distinct tasks require multiple experts and manual switching. Reinforcement-learning controllers also often approximate actuator capability with constant torque limits, ignoring the decline in DC-motor torque at high speed and, for some joints, position-dependent limits. This can produce simulation behaviors that cause real motor faults.

The paper asks how heterogeneous skills can be learned in one policy, selected automatically, and executed near hardware limits without violating actuator feasibility.

## Main contribution

- Joint learning of omnidirectional locomotion, high-platform climbing, and fall recovery in one low-level policy.
- Skill indicators that disambiguate heterogeneous objectives.
- Hard DC-motor constraints dependent on velocity and joint position.
- A proprioception-only high-level selector for autonomous skill transitions.

## Methodology

![MUJICA unified multi-skill policy with state estimation, skill selection, and constraint critics.](/reviews/2026-07-22-MUJICA/fig1-framework.png)

*Figure 1. MUJICA unified multi-skill policy with state estimation, skill selection, and constraint critics.*

A state estimator predicts base velocity, wheel-ground distance, body-part collision, and latent embodiment state from proprioceptive history. The actor combines these estimates with current observation and a skill indicator to command legs and wheels. Reward and constraint critics evaluate task return and safety violations, and the policy is optimized with P3O.

In stage S1, the low-level policy is trained with explicit skill indicators. The policy is then frozen, and stage S2 trains a selector using a unified velocity-tracking objective. DC-motor limits follow manufacturer operating regions: torque is constant at low speed, decreases linearly at high speed, and includes a joint-position-dependent cosine term where appropriate.

## Experimental setup

![Real-world stair recovery, 20 cm stair ascent, and 80 cm platform climbing.](/reviews/2026-07-22-MUJICA/fig3-real-world-skills.png)

*Figure 2. Real-world stair recovery, 20 cm stair ascent, and 80 cm platform climbing.*

Training uses 4,096 Isaac Lab environments, with 30,000 low-level and 10,000 selector iterations. Policies run at 50 Hz. The curriculum covers stairs, slopes, discrete and rough terrain, and deep pits; domain randomization includes friction, mass, external forces, pushes, and motor gains.

Comparisons cover estimator ablations, DreamWaQ+P3O, vanilla PPO, no skill indicator, and no motor constraints. Hardware experiments use Unitree Go2-W.

## Key results

![Torque-velocity distributions before and after enforcing DC-motor constraints.](/reviews/2026-07-22-MUJICA/fig2-motor-constraint-results.png)

*Figure 3. Torque-velocity distributions before and after enforcing DC-motor constraints.*

Without motor constraints, more than 90% of thigh torque-speed samples lie outside the feasible region; constraints reduce violations below `3.5%`. In sequential stair recovery, stair climbing, and platform climbing, the full selector achieves stage success rates of `95%, 95%, and 91%`; a no-indicator method with separate rewards reaches only `72%, 72%, and 38%`.

The real Go2-W recovers on 30-degree stairs, climbs 20 cm stairs, and surmounts an 80 cm outdoor and 1 m indoor platform without triggering over-current or thermal protection.

## Strengths

- The policy unifies dynamically different skills rather than only related gaits.
- Motor-feasible modeling has clear engineering value.
- Skill-indicator and selector ablations are informative.
- Hardware demonstrations approach embodiment and actuator limits.

## Weaknesses

- The skill set is predefined and limited.
- Proprioceptive selection recognizes terrain demand only after interaction.
- Two-stage learning still requires manual tasks, indicators, and curriculum design.

## Discussion questions

1. Can a continuous skill latent replace discrete indicators?
2. How should motor thermal and lifetime constraints extend the current feasible region?
3. How can safety be guaranteed when the high-level selector misclassifies a situation?
4. Can manipulation and locomotion skills share the same architecture?

## Relevance to our lab

MUJICA aligns with a body-cerebellum-brain hierarchy: the low level models hardware constraints, the middle layer unifies motor skills, and the high level selects behavior. Its motor modeling is directly relevant to safe, high-performance industrial wheeled humanoids.

## Possible follow-up ideas

- Connect visual or VLA task understanding to skill selection.
- Add motor thermal, lifetime, and impact constraints.
- Extend to manipulation, transport, and whole-body skills.
- Investigate automatic skill discovery and online skill expansion.
