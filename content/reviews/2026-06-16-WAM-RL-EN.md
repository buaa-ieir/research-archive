## One-sentence summary

WAM-RL extends reinforcement learning for World-Action models beyond actor-only tuning: it updates the world model through online video SFT on successful trajectories and trains the actor with reconstruction rewards that align imagined and executed futures.

![WAM-RL overview](/reviews/2026-06-16-WAM-RL-EN/fig1-overview.png)

*Figure 1. Prior RL methods mainly optimize the actor, while WAM-RL jointly adapts the world model and actor through online interaction.*

## Problem addressed

World-Action (WA) models jointly model future visual states and actions. Unlike standard VLA policies, they explicitly ask what the world will look like if a candidate action sequence is taken. This predictive structure is attractive for long-horizon manipulation.

However, most WA models are trained from expert trajectories using supervised learning. This limits skill acquisition beyond the demonstration distribution and prevents the model from improving through online interaction. Naively applying RL is also difficult. A WA model has two tightly coupled components: a world model that predicts future observations and an actor that turns predictions into actions. Optimizing only the actor cannot fix accumulated world-model errors, while unconstrained world-model finetuning can shift the latent space that the actor depends on.

The paper asks: **how can reinforcement learning stably optimize both components of a World-Action model?**

## Main contribution

The paper contributes:

- **WAM-RL**, the first RL framework explicitly designed for the World-Action paradigm;
- online video self-supervised finetuning for the world model using successful trajectories;
- KL regularization to preserve latent-space stability during world-model adaptation;
- a reconstruction-based reward that trains the actor by comparing imagined and executed outcomes;
- experiments showing that actor-only RL is insufficient for long-horizon tasks.

## Methodology

WAM-RL is built on the view that the world model is the main source of predictive capability, while the actor translates latent predictions into executable control. Therefore, improving the actor alone is not enough if the world model cannot represent failure and recovery dynamics.

The training pipeline has two coupled parts:

1. **World-model update**: successful online trajectories are used for video SFT, with a KL term keeping the current latent distribution close to the frozen pretrained model;
2. **Actor update**: after the actor executes actions, the resulting observations are compared with imagined future observations, producing a reconstruction reward for policy-gradient optimization.

This gives the actor a denser signal than sparse success/failure reward while keeping it aligned with the world model’s predictive structure.

## Experiments and results

![Main results](/reviews/2026-06-16-WAM-RL-EN/fig2-main-results.png)

*Figure 2. WAM-RL improves over both the pretrained base model and actor-only RL on LIBERO-Object and RLBench Water Plants.*

The main table is compact but informative:

- on **LIBERO-Object**, the base model obtains 68%, actor-only RL reaches 78%, and WAM-RL reaches **82%**;
- on **RLBench Water Plants**, the base model obtains 19%, actor-only RL drops slightly to 18%, and WAM-RL improves to **22%**.

This directly supports the paper’s thesis: actor-only RL can help short-horizon tasks, but long-horizon improvement requires updating the world model as well.

![Reconstruction reward ablation](/reviews/2026-06-16-WAM-RL-EN/fig3-reconstruction-reward.png)

*Figure 3. Different reconstruction objectives produce different reward distributions and downstream performance; Pixel MSE achieves the best reported success rate.*

The reward ablation is interesting. Optical-flow reward separates successful and failed trajectories more strongly, but it does not yield the best policy. Pixel MSE is less discriminative but better aligned with the world model’s own video-training objective, which likely makes it a more stable actor-training signal.

![Online video SFT qualitative comparison](/reviews/2026-06-16-WAM-RL-EN/fig4-video-sft-comparison.png)

*Figure 4. Without video SFT, the world model keeps predicting an erroneous future after a failed grasp; with video SFT, it imagines recovery behavior and re-attempts.*

The qualitative comparison is the most compelling part of the paper. Video SFT changes the model’s predicted future after failure: instead of drifting out of distribution, it predicts corrective behavior such as re-positioning and re-grasping.

## Strengths

- Correctly identifies that WA-model RL cannot be treated as ordinary actor-only policy optimization;
- clean separation between world-model adaptation and actor RL;
- reconstruction reward matches the mechanism of World-Action models;
- compact experiments are well aligned with the main hypothesis;
- useful insight that actor-only RL is limited for long-horizon tasks.

## Weaknesses

- evaluation is small, covering only a limited set of LIBERO and RLBench tasks;
- using only successful trajectories for world-model SFT may bias adaptation;
- Pixel MSE can be sensitive to visually irrelevant changes;
- online world-model updates need more validation on larger models and real robots;
- comparison with stronger VLA-RL and model-based RL baselines remains limited.

## Discussion questions

1. Should failed trajectories also be used to learn recovery dynamics?
2. Can reconstruction reward encourage actions that merely match prediction rather than maximize task success?
3. Is Pixel MSE still appropriate for multi-view or real-robot observations?
4. Do WA models need explicit uncertainty estimates during online RL?
5. How should actor and world-model update frequencies be scheduled in long-horizon tasks?

## Relation to our lab

WAM-RL is directly relevant to embodied world models, VLA policies, robot imitation learning, and online RL. Its main lesson is that once a policy depends on imagined futures, reinforcement learning should maintain the world model’s executable predictive structure, not only tune the action head. This is a useful starting point for any work on video-prediction-based robot policies.

## Possible follow-up directions

- test WAM-RL in real-robot online finetuning;
- design more robust semantic or geometric reconstruction rewards;
- use failed trajectories to model recovery dynamics;
- study alternating update schedules for actor and world model;
- combine the framework with diffusion policies, VLA action heads, or human-intervention data.
