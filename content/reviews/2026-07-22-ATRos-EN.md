## One-sentence summary

ATRos learns when to favor rolling and when to coordinate wheels and legs, aiming to preserve obstacle capability while improving energy efficiency.

## Problem addressed

Wheeled-legged robots combine continuous rolling dynamics, nonholonomic constraints, and intermittent ground contacts, creating a larger action space than pure quadrupeds. Without specialized design, a learned policy may overuse leg motion and waste energy or overuse wheels and lose terrain adaptability.

The paper asks how proprioception-only control can coordinate rolling and stepping without predefined gaits while balancing stability, agility, and energy consumption.

## Main contribution

- A proprioceptive PRPN-PPO architecture for hybrid locomotion.
- Wheel-related and energy-aware rewards that encourage efficient behavior.
- Joint output of leg positions and wheel velocities without predefined gait switching.
- Preliminary zero-shot validation on grass and stairs with Unitree Go2-W.

## Methodology

![ATRos predictive-response policy training and zero-shot deployment pipeline.](/reviews/2026-07-22-ATRos-EN/fig1-framework.png)

*Figure 1. ATRos predictive-response policy training and zero-shot deployment pipeline.*

ATRos formulates locomotion as a POMDP. The Predictive Response Policy Network encodes observation history to estimate body velocity and latent environmental features. The actor combines these estimates with a 57-dimensional current observation and outputs 12 leg-position increments and four wheel velocities. The critic receives privileged state during training.

Training uses 4,096 Isaac Gym environments, domain randomization, and terrain curriculum. Randomized parameters include mass, center of mass, payload, surface properties, motor strength, PD gains, delay, and external disturbances. The curriculum contains 200 height-field terrain variants and progressively expands command range. The hardware policy runs at 50 Hz.

## Experimental setup

The hardware platform is a 16-DoF Unitree Go2-W EDU, with the policy executed on an RTX 4090. Tests cover soft uneven grass, natural slopes, and 13 cm stairs. The study observes wheel-dominant travel, stair descent, and forward/backward stair ascent.

## Key results

![Real-world traversal on grass and stairs in descending, forward-ascent, and backward-ascent modes.](/reviews/2026-07-22-ATRos-EN/fig2-real-world-locomotion.png)

*Figure 2. Real-world traversal on grass and stairs in descending, forward-ascent, and backward-ascent modes.*

The robot traverses grass and small slopes primarily using wheels, descends stairs by rolling, and develops coordinated wheel-leg actions for forward and backward ascent of 13 cm stairs. The authors report terrain generalization and improved efficiency on rolling-dominant surfaces.

However, the paper provides no numerical power, CoT, success-rate, or baseline-comparison table. The energy-efficiency claim is therefore supported mainly by reward design and qualitative demonstrations.

## Strengths

- The architecture cleanly combines history-based estimation and hybrid action generation.
- No predefined gait or manual rolling-stepping switch is required.
- Domain randomization and curriculum coverage are broad.
- Hardware demonstrations include both forward and backward stair ascent.

## Weaknesses

- The main energy claim lacks quantitative energy or CoT results.
- No controlled hybrid-locomotion baseline is reported.
- PRPN and wheel-reward ablations are absent.
- Real-world terrain and task coverage are limited.

## Discussion questions

1. Should energy efficiency be measured by electrical energy, mechanical power, or CoT?
2. What is PRPN's independent benefit over a standard history encoder?
3. Can energy penalties make the policy overly conservative on difficult terrain?
4. How should the quality of rolling-stepping transitions be evaluated?

## Relevance to our lab

This work is relevant to wheeled humanoid control, reinforcement-learning locomotion, and long-endurance mobile bodies. It provides a useful starting architecture, but industrial deployment would require stronger evidence on energy, thermal stability, payload, and reliability.

## Possible follow-up ideas

- Measure electrical energy, mechanical energy, and CoT systematically.
- Compare with pure rolling, fixed gaits, and existing RL controllers.
- Ablate PRPN, reward terms, and observation-history length.
- Evaluate long-distance operation, payloads, and more complex obstacles.
