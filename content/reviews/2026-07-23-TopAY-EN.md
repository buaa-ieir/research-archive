## One-sentence summary

TopAY searches multiple topologically distinct base paths, samples manipulator states in parallel, and optimizes an arc-length–yaw trajectory representation, addressing both high-dimensional whole-body search and differential-drive singularities near zero velocity.

![Complex-environment planning and arc-length–yaw parameterization in TopAY.](/reviews/2026-07-23-TopAY-EN/fig1-planning-motivation.png)

*Figure 1. Complex-environment planning and arc-length–yaw parameterization in TopAY.*

## Problem addressed

Differential-drive mobile manipulators combine base mobility with manipulation but create a high-dimensional state space. Direct whole-body sampling or single-initialization optimization often fails in dense obstacles and narrow passages. The nonholonomic base also makes conventional Cartesian differential-flatness representations singular near stopping or in-place rotation.

The paper asks how structured initialization can reduce whole-body planning complexity and how the trajectory can be parameterized to support stopping, reversing, and turning while satisfying base kinematics, manipulator limits, end-effector targets, and whole-body collision constraints.

## Main contribution

- A hierarchical initialization pipeline that searches multi-topology base paths before parallel manipulator-state sampling.
- Visibility PRM, DFS, path shortening, and UVD pruning for distinct obstacle-avoidance classes.
- Arc-length `s(t)` and yaw `θ(t)` parameterization that avoids zero-velocity singularities.
- Piecewise quintic-polynomial representation of base arc length, base yaw, and manipulator joints.
- Jerk-and-time trajectory optimization with terminal-pose, feasibility, environmental-collision, and self-collision constraints.
- Multi-threaded sampling and optimization, validated against REMANI in simulation and physical experiments.

## Methodology

![TopAY pipeline for topological search, manipulator sampling, and trajectory optimization.](/reviews/2026-07-23-TopAY-EN/fig2-framework.png)

*Figure 2. TopAY pipeline for topological search, manipulator sampling, and trajectory optimization.*

Given a target end-effector pose in SE(3), TopAY builds a 2D base roadmap using visibility PRM. DFS, shortcutting, and UVD classification produce topologically distinct paths. Each path is discretized into SE(2) base states, and a constrained Bi-RRT*/RRT-Connect-style sampler searches manipulator configurations conditioned on those states instead of sampling blindly in the full whole-body space.

Candidate paths are optimized in parallel. Base motion is represented by arc length and yaw, while manipulator motion is represented by joint polynomials. A PHR-ALM solver handles smoothness, duration, terminal constraints, feasibility, and collision costs. During physical deployment, early termination accepts the first successful result after a short grace period for potentially shorter alternatives.

## Experimental setup

![TopAY physical platform, perception stack, and planning-control system.](/reviews/2026-07-23-TopAY-EN/fig3-system-platform.png)

*Figure 3. TopAY physical platform, perception stack, and planning-control system.*

The physical system combines an AgileX Tracer differential-drive base, a Realman RM75-6F arm, a Wheeltec gripper, a Livox Mid-360 LiDAR, and an Intel NUC 11. FAST-LIO2 provides localization, ROG-Map maintains an online 3D grid map and SDF, and an MPC controller outputs base linear/angular velocity and arm joint velocity.

Physical evaluation uses pick-and-place in an initially unknown indoor environment, including replanning after newly introduced obstacles. Simulation contains 20 m × 20 m Cuboids and Tables scenes; the latter creates narrow passages with table legs. Ablations include JPSOnly and a nonparallel Sequential variant, followed by comparison with REMANI.

## Key results

![Physical pick-and-place, online replanning, and avoidance of newly introduced obstacles.](/reviews/2026-07-23-TopAY-EN/fig4-real-world-results.png)

*Figure 4. Physical pick-and-place, online replanning, and avoidance of newly introduced obstacles.*

TopAY completes physical indoor pick-and-place tasks and replans when new obstacles block the current trajectory. In the Tables scene, parallel TopAY averages `312.0 ms`, compared with `1363 ms` for Sequential, showing the practical benefit of concurrent initialization and optimization.

Against REMANI, success rates in Cuboids-Large are `97.0%` versus `71.1%`; in Tables-Large they are `61.8%` versus `11.4%`. Average Tables-Large planning time is `794.7 ms` for TopAY and `4442 ms` for REMANI. Multi-topology initialization, parallel processing, and arc-length–yaw parameterization jointly improve planning success and efficiency.

## Strengths

- Directly targets both high-dimensional search and differential-drive nonholonomic constraints.
- Supplies structurally diverse initializations to nonconvex optimization.
- Handles stopping, reversing, and in-place rotation without Cartesian singularities.
- Balances candidate diversity with practical latency through parallelization.
- Includes physical experiments, ablations, and systematic comparison with REMANI.
- Demonstrates online mapping and replanning in initially unknown environments.

## Weaknesses

- Hierarchical base-arm planning remains partly decoupled and lacks completeness guarantees.
- Success in the hardest Tables-Large scene remains `61.8%`.
- Performance depends on whether base paths and manipulator sampling cover a feasible region.
- Evaluation is limited to a single-arm differential-drive platform.
- Contact force, grasp stability, and manipulated-object dynamics are not modeled.
- Dynamic adaptation relies on detection followed by replanning rather than continuous predictive avoidance.

## Discussion questions

1. Are 2D base topology classes sufficient to express whole-body feasibility in complex 3D scenes?
2. When a base path is feasible but arm posture is not, should base and arm be replanned jointly?
3. Can arc-length–yaw parameterization extend to omnidirectional, four-wheel-steering, or legged platforms?
4. How should bimanual relative-pose and coordination constraints be incorporated?
5. How should a carried object enter SDF, self-collision, and passage-feasibility checks?
6. Can learned models rank topology classes or predict better manipulator sampling distributions?

## Relevance to our lab

The work is closely related to mobile-manipulator whole-body collision avoidance, trajectory optimization, online replanning, and model-predictive control. Its central insight is to provide nonconvex optimization with structurally distinct topological initializations and a numerically stable, platform-aware trajectory parameterization. These ideas transfer naturally to mobile bimanual and humanoid planning.

## Possible follow-up ideas

- Extend multi-topology initialization to mobile bimanual and humanoid systems.
- Add relative end-effector pose, contact-force, and grasp-stability constraints.
- Treat carried objects as extended links or attached collision bodies.
- Learn topology ranking and conditional manipulator-sampling distributions.
- Combine arc-length–yaw planning with a hierarchical MPC/WBC controller.
- Add motion prediction and risk constraints for dynamic obstacles.
