## One-sentence summary

SAPS lets a pretrained VLA handle nominal manipulation while a human supplies occasional directional corrections, approaching teleoperation reliability with much less operator effort.

![SAPS motivation](/reviews/2026-06-13-SAPS-EN/fig1-motivation.png)

*Figure 1. Human input steers an out-of-distribution VLA rollout back toward a recoverable region before autonomy resumes.*

## Problem addressed

Generalist VLAs remain brittle to changes in object placement, appearance, and language goals. Pure teleoperation is reliable but demands continuous precise control, while many inference-time steering methods require auxiliary dynamics models, sampler modifications, or reasoning modules. SAPS asks whether post-inference action blending can provide a lightweight, model-agnostic alternative.

## Main contribution

- A training-free action-level shared-autonomy framework;
- three arbitration rules: full takeover, fixed 50/50 blending, and dynamic cosine-similarity blending;
- evaluation on LIBERO, LIBERO-PRO, CALVIN, and a physical Franka arm;
- joint analysis of success, completion time, and human intervention.

## Methodology

The VLA and operator each produce a seven-dimensional end-effector command. With no human input, autonomy weight α is one. During intervention, Takeover sets α to zero, Blending uses α=0.5, and Cosine maps directional agreement between human and policy actions through a sigmoid. Gripper commands are handled separately so either source can initiate grasping.

![SAPS framework](/reviews/2026-06-13-SAPS-EN/fig2-framework.png)

*Figure 2. SAPS is placed after policy inference and can swap arbitration rules without modifying the VLA.*

## Experiments and key results

For large LIBERO spatial perturbations, π0.5 falls to 22.9% success, while fixed and cosine blending retain 87.9% and 90.0%. Across ten LIBERO-PRO tasks, autonomous π0.5 averages 15.0%; Blending, Takeover, and Cosine reach 92.6%, 93.2%, and 97.4%, near 98.8% teleoperation. Their mean intervention rates are only 10.8%, 11.7%, and 30.0%.

![LIBERO results](/reviews/2026-06-13-SAPS-EN/fig3-libero-results.png)

*Figure 3. Shared autonomy maintains high success as spatial perturbations increase and does so with limited intervention.*

On CALVIN long-horizon chains, Cosine raises subtask success from 91.27% to 94.85% and five-task completion from 63.33% to 76.67%, with human input on 13.73% of steps. With a weaker Diffusion Policy, Cosine averages 93% across eleven subtasks, versus 45% for the base policy, 66% for ITPS, and 80% for DynaGuide.

![CALVIN results](/reviews/2026-06-13-SAPS-EN/fig4-calvin-results.png)

*Figure 4. Cosine arbitration improves both block lifting and articulated-object manipulation on CALVIN.*

On three real tasks, π0.5 averages about 26.7% success, Blending 93.3%, and Cosine 98.3%. Shared autonomy also reduces human input by 30–50% relative to teleoperation.

## Strengths

- Truly model-agnostic post-inference integration.
- Reports operator effort and speed in addition to success.
- Tests both a strong VLA and a lower-capacity diffusion policy.
- Exploits a natural complement between policy skill priors and human anomaly recognition.

## Weaknesses

- Requires a live operator at deployment.
- Cosine agreement is not equivalent to uncertainty, task progress, or safety.
- Simple blending may fail when both human and policy are wrong.
- Most within-benchmark trials use one operator, limiting conclusions about user variability.
- Robot embodiments and physical tasks remain limited.

## Discussion questions

1. Should authority depend on action agreement, uncertainty, or contact risk?
2. Can interventions become training data for later policy improvement?
3. Does continuous blending make the executed action harder for users to predict?
4. Should translation, rotation, and gripper control use separate arbitration weights?
5. When is hard takeover preferable to smooth blending?

## Relevance to our lab

SAPS fits naturally with glove teleoperation and robot-control systems. The glove need not command the entire trajectory; it can instead provide corrections while a VLA supplies nominal motion. Agreement-based arbitration can also be extended to joint angles, force, and tactile feedback for rapid intervention during slip or pose errors.

## Possible follow-up ideas

- Add tactile, torque, and collision predictions to arbitration.
- Learn user-specific blending policies.
- Convert intervention segments into recovery demonstrations.
- Introduce hysteresis and stability guarantees for mode switching.
- Allocate authority separately across arms, fingers, or joints.
