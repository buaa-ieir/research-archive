## One-sentence summary

LEGS combines real-scene 3D Gaussian Splatting for appearance, MuJoCo and whole-body control for execution, and procedural motion generation to fine-tune humanoid VLAs without human demonstrations.

![LEGS overview](/reviews/2026-05-31-LEGS-EN/fig1-overview.png)

*Figure 1. The pipeline runs from scene/object reconstruction through synthetic data generation and VLA fine-tuning to physical G1 deployment.*

## Problem addressed

Humanoid loco-manipulation data are expensive because teleoperation consumes robot time, operator labor, and physical space, and appearance changes require recollection. Conventional mesh simulation introduces a visual gap that prevents reliable zero-shot transfer. LEGS asks whether photorealistic hybrid simulation can replace teleoperation, seed demonstrations, human video, and task rewards.

## Main contribution

- A hybrid simulator with static 3DGS backgrounds and dynamic mesh foregrounds;
- MuJoCo dynamics and the same SONIC controller used during deployment;
- procedural Walk, Pick, and Place primitives with success verification;
- two-stage color calibration across scan, mesh, and deployment-camera color spaces;
- motion/appearance decoupling for inexpensive rerendering;
- 1,110 trials with ψ0, π0.5, and GR00T N1.6 on Unitree G1.

## Methodology

Handheld video is reconstructed into a static 3DGS background, while SAM3D produces manipulable object meshes. Robot and objects interact in MuJoCo under 18D SONIC commands; dynamic meshes are depth-composited over the Gaussian background.

![LEGS pipeline](/reviews/2026-05-31-LEGS-EN/fig2-pipeline.png)

*Figure 2. Separating rendering and physics allows the same procedural motion to be rerendered for different VLA backbones and appearances.*

Color calibration first rescales object albedo inside masks, then fits a global 3×3 ColorChecker transform to the RealSense deployment camera.

![Rendering comparison](/reviews/2026-05-31-LEGS-EN/fig3-rendering-comparison.png)

*Figure 3. Mesh-only, uncalibrated LEGS, calibrated LEGS, and physical-camera views.*

## Experiments and key results

Three tasks progress from stationary pick-and-place to walking, turning, squatting, and placing on a low table. Each data/backbone/task setting has ten trials.

![Three tasks](/reviews/2026-05-31-LEGS-EN/fig4-tasks.png)

*Figure 4. Increasing whole-body difficulty across manipulation and loco-manipulation tasks.*

LEGS(200) is best or tied across all nine backbone-task combinations. Budget-matched LEGS(50) never underperforms Teleop(50). On the hardest task, all teleoperation-trained policies score 0/10, while LEGS(200) obtains 5/10, 2/10, and 6/10. Mesh-only SAM3D(200) averages 33% success versus 67% for LEGS(200), highlighting the rendering stack.

Under combined object and scene shift, unaugmented Teleop, SAM3D, and LEGS fall to 0–1/10. Rerendered LEGS-AUG(200) reaches 100%, 80%, and 40% across tasks, compared with 60%, 50%, and 20% for SAM3D-AUG. A new appearance condition costs about 0.1 GPU-hours rather than more than 1.5 operator-hours.

## Strengths

- Clean rendering ablation under a shared dynamics/control pipeline.
- Consistent findings across three VLA backbones.
- Budget matching rules out data quantity as the sole explanation.
- Motion-appearance decoupling creates a practical parallelizable adaptation workflow.
- Evaluates genuine whole-body long-horizon behavior.

## Weaknesses

- Static 3DGS struggles with transparent, reflective, or dynamic scenes.
- Fixed exposure and white balance create lighting sensitivity.
- New scenes still require a one-to-two-minute scan.
- Procedural primitives are feasible but not dynamically optimal.
- Only one humanoid, camera, and controller are tested.
- Ten trials per setting leave substantial uncertainty.

## Discussion questions

1. How much gain comes from 3DGS, calibration, or procedural diversity?
2. How should static Gaussian scenes update around moving people and objects?
3. Does success-only procedural data omit states needed for recovery?
4. Can touch and contact dynamics enter the same data engine?
5. When does rerendering one motion cease to be valid for a new object geometry?

## Relevance to our lab

LEGS suggests separating robot motion from scene appearance. Glove-recorded or procedural hand motions could be replayed in simulation under varied cameras, objects, and workspaces. For XHand, joint trajectories can be decoupled from visual backgrounds and tested across appearance conditions before physical collection.

## Possible follow-up ideas

- Add failed trajectories and recovery primitives.
- Use dynamic 3DGS or online scene updates.
- Jointly render vision, depth, touch, and force.
- Validate across humanoids and low-level controllers.
- Use uncertainty to prioritize synthetic distributions for physical testing.
