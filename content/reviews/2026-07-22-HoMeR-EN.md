## One-sentence summary

HoMeR uses absolute keyposes for large-range motion, relative dense actions for local precision, and a whole-body controller for base-arm coordination, enabling real-world household manipulation from only 20 demonstrations per task.

## Problem addressed

Mobile manipulators extend the workspace of fixed-base arms but create a high-dimensional coordination problem. Learning base poses and arm joints directly requires demonstrations to cover many possible whole-body coordination patterns, while using only incremental actions over long distances causes accumulated error and trajectory drift.

Household tasks are also intrinsically multi-stage: approaching an object requires stable large-range motion, whereas grasping, alignment, pulling, and sweeping require continuous and reactive local control. The paper asks how these action representations can be combined within an automatically switching policy while delegating low-level coordination to a model-based controller.

## Main contribution

- A 6-DoF end-effector action space whose commands are converted into coordinated holonomic-base and 7-DoF arm motion by a kinematic whole-body controller.
- A hybrid imitation-learning framework containing keypose and dense-action sub-policies.
- Joint prediction of the end-effector action and next control mode, enabling automatic keypose, dense, and termination transitions.
- Absolute keypose prediction through point-cloud saliency and 3D offsets, plus HoMeR-COND for conditioning on VLM-generated target keypoints.
- An iPhone-based whole-body teleoperation interface and evaluation on three simulated and three real household tasks.

## Methodology

![HoMeR data collection, hybrid-policy execution, and whole-body control.](/reviews/2026-07-23-HoMeR-EN/fig1-overview.png)

*Figure 1. HoMeR data collection, hybrid-policy execution, and whole-body control.*

HoMeR separates task-level learning from low-level whole-body control. The policy predicts targets only in end-effector space; constrained inverse kinematics coordinates the base and arm while handling joint limits, posture regularization, and optional internal-collision constraints.

![Hybrid imitation-learning architecture combining keypose, dense, and whole-body-control modules.](/reviews/2026-07-23-HoMeR-EN/fig2-policy-architecture.png)

*Figure 2. Hybrid imitation-learning architecture combining keypose, dense, and whole-body-control modules.*

The keypose policy predicts a salient point and 3D offset from a scene point cloud to produce absolute poses for long-range reaching. The dense policy uses Diffusion Policy on base and wrist RGB observations to generate relative end-effector increments for alignment and contact-rich manipulation. Both sub-policies predict the next mode. HoMeR-COND additionally back-projects a VLM-selected image point into 3D, improving target specification under novel appearances and clutter.

## Experimental setup

![TidyBot++ mobile-manipulation platform used for household experiments.](/reviews/2026-07-23-HoMeR-EN/fig3-platform.png)

*Figure 3. TidyBot++ mobile-manipulation platform used for household experiments.*

TidyBot++ combines a holonomic mobile base, a 7-DoF Kinova arm, two base-mounted scene cameras, a wrist fisheye camera, an onboard NUC, and a GPU laptop. Demonstrations are collected through iPhone end-effector teleoperation while the controller coordinates the base and arm.

Evaluation covers the simulated Cube, Dishwasher, and Cabinet tasks and the real-world Pillow, TV Remote, and Sweep Trash tasks, with 20 demonstrations per task. Baselines independently vary the action representation—dense or hybrid—and the controller—decoupled base-arm control or whole-body control.

## Key results

![Task success counts for HoMeR and action/control-structure baselines.](/reviews/2026-07-23-HoMeR-EN/fig4-task-results.png)

*Figure 4. Task success counts for HoMeR and action/control-structure baselines.*

HoMeR succeeds in `95/120` trials, achieving an overall success rate of `79.17%` and the highest success count on every task. It exceeds the second-best baseline by an average of `29.17` percentage points. Keyposes reduce long-horizon incremental drift, dense actions retain local precision, and whole-body control avoids failures caused by independently positioned bases and unreachable or unfavorable arm configurations.

Under changes in object size, distractors, and appearance, standard HoMeR degrades away from the training distribution. HoMeR-COND, combined with color removal and distractor-point augmentation, improves generalization through externally specified target keypoints.

## Strengths

- Reduces high-dimensional mobile-manipulation learning to low-dimensional task decisions.
- Aligns the hybrid action representation with the phase structure of real tasks.
- Separately evaluates the contributions of action representation and whole-body control.
- Demonstrates strong sample efficiency with 20 demonstrations per task.
- Reuses a relatively task-independent controller across tasks.
- Includes both simulation and real household experiments.

## Weaknesses

- The controller does not handle collisions with furniture, walls, or clutter.
- The kinematic formulation omits dynamics, contact force, friction, and object dynamics.
- The study focuses on the manipulation “last mile,” not full navigation-manipulation integration.
- Policies remain task-specific, and mode intervals and salient points require annotation.
- Evaluation is limited to a single-arm holonomic platform.
- Pose-only actions are insufficient for sustained force-regulation tasks.

## Discussion questions

1. How can external collision constraints be added to the controller without sacrificing real-time performance?
2. Can action modes be discovered from task progress and contact state rather than annotated?
3. How should force or impedance control be integrated for wiping, insertion, and pushing?
4. Can HoMeR scale to bimanual mobile systems while maintaining relative end-effector constraints?
5. How can VLM-selected 2D points be converted robustly to 3D under occlusion or missing depth?

## Relevance to our lab

The paper is directly relevant to mobile manipulation, whole-body coordination, imitation learning, and embodied-data collection. Its central lesson is to use an analytical controller as a structural prior: the policy handles low-dimensional task decisions, while the controller manages constraints and redundant degrees of freedom, reducing data requirements and improving interpretability.

## Possible follow-up ideas

- Integrate RGB-D, point-cloud, or ESDF environmental constraints into whole-body control.
- Replace manual keypose and phase labels with learned mode switching.
- Extend the system to mobile bimanual, differential-drive, and humanoid platforms.
- Treat manipulated objects as attached collision bodies.
- Combine keyposes, dense actions, and force control in a three-level hybrid policy.
- Train shared multi-task policies and evaluate cross-home generalization.
