## One-sentence summary

RL4IL uses reinforcement-learning-guided retrieval, soft fusion, and soft imputation to keep imitation policies functional when one or more sensor modalities are missing, without retraining the main policy network.

![Main dropout result](/reviews/2026-06-13-RL4IL-EN/fig1-dropout-results.png)

*Figure 1. RL4IL substantially outperforms strong baselines under complete camera dropout across LIBERO suites.*

## Problem addressed

Most multimodal imitation-learning methods assume that all input modalities remain available at deployment. In the real world, cameras can fail, become occluded, or temporarily drop out. Prior missing-modality methods often require retraining or fine-tuning for each new failure pattern, which is impractical for robotic deployment. The paper asks whether a robot can remain robust to modality failure by relying on a demonstration library and a retrieval mechanism instead of retraining the control policy.

## Main contributions

- Introduces RL-guided demonstration retrieval using PPO over candidate sets;
- Uses soft cross-attention fusion over top-ranked demonstrations rather than hard single-neighbor selection;
- Uses a modality-specific soft imputation head to reconstruct missing embeddings from donor demonstrations;
- Demonstrates strong robustness on LIBERO-Goal, LIBERO-Object, and LIBERO-Spatial under complete camera dropout.

## Methodology

RL4IL proceeds in three stages. First, frozen pretrained encoders produce an embedding for each modality, and a modality-fair distance is used so that high-dimensional modalities do not dominate nearest-neighbor retrieval. Second, a learned RL policy reranks candidate demonstrations. Third, the top-ranked candidates are softly fused to predict the action. If a modality is missing, a dedicated retriever selects donor demonstrations and a soft imputation head reconstructs the missing embedding before the same downstream retrieval-fusion process is applied.

What is interesting here is that robustness is not pushed into a monolithic end-to-end policy. Instead, it is built into the retrieval and representation-repair layer, which makes zero-shot deployment under sensor failure much more practical.

## Experiments and main results

The main result is striking. Under complete camera dropout, RL4IL reaches about 0.733 success on LIBERO-Object, whereas the strongest prior baseline DisDP reaches only 0.295. On LIBERO-Goal it also reaches about 0.700, clearly above BC, DisBC, and BESO-based baselines.

![Normalization ablation](/reviews/2026-06-13-RL4IL-EN/fig2-normalization-ablation.png)

*Figure 2. Modality-fair normalization is important for reliable retrieval and prevents dominant embeddings from biasing the neighbor search.*

The training-epoch study shows that RL4IL is already competitive very early and then improves steadily, suggesting that the retrieval-based design does not depend on very long training.

![Epoch study](/reviews/2026-06-13-RL4IL-EN/fig3-epoch-study.png)

*Figure 3. RL4IL remains competitive across training epochs and does not require excessive training to work well.*

Finally, the top-K ablation indicates that soft fusion is fairly robust to the number of retrieved candidates, which is encouraging from a practical tuning perspective.

## Strengths

- Studies a very practical deployment problem: sensor dropout;
- Avoids retraining the full policy for each missing-modality pattern;
- Delivers large gains with clear ablations;
- Retrieval, fusion, and imputation each have an interpretable role.

## Limitations

- Evaluation mainly focuses on complete camera dropout;
- The method depends strongly on demonstration-library coverage;
- Using frozen encoders may cap the quality of the representation space;
- The paper emphasizes robustness more than peak performance under no-missing-modality settings.

## Discussion questions

1. What happens when the test observation is far outside the support of the demonstration library?
2. Can soft imputation be combined with generative observation-completion models?
3. Should the retrieval policy use longer temporal context rather than stepwise candidate ranking?
4. Can the same framework handle missing language, tactile, or proprioceptive signals?
5. Will retrieval latency and memory usage become a bottleneck in real-time control?

## Relation to our lab

This work is relevant to any robot system that depends on multiple sensors. If a lab studies vision, proprioception, or tactile inputs jointly, then robustness to partial sensor failure becomes a serious deployment concern. RL4IL suggests a useful idea: instead of putting all robustness into policy weights, keep part of the recovery mechanism in a retrievable experience library.

## Possible follow-up directions

- Extend from step-level to trajectory-level retrieval;
- Allow the demonstration memory to update online;
- Study joint missing-modality patterns rather than single-camera dropout;
- Combine retrieval with stronger policy backbones such as diffusion or VLA models;
- Tie retrieval confidence to safety monitoring and human override mechanisms.
