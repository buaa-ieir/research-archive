## One-sentence summary

FR-Net lets a recovery policy reason explicitly about how robot mass is distributed and which body parts are in contact, improving safety on steep stairs, gaps, and suspended beams.

## Problem addressed

Recovery on complex terrain requires more than orienting the base upright: the controller must infer mass distribution, support regions, and body contacts. Policies based only on implicit context may fail around hidden gaps and beams, while aggressive rolling can cause secondary falls or hardware damage.

The paper investigates whether explicit morphology and contact prediction from limited proprioception can support safe recovery across robot bodies and terrain types.

## Main contribution

- A Mass-Contact Predictor (MCP) estimating segment mass, collision probability, and latent dynamics.
- Stability rewards that constrain displacement, contact force, and dangerous rolling.
- Training and evaluation across randomized Go2, Spot, Lite3, Aliengo, and Go1 morphologies.
- Real Go2 validation and a simulated extension to Unitree G1 humanoid recovery.

## Methodology

![FR-Net policy training, mass-contact prediction, and cross-platform deployment framework.](/reviews/2026-07-22-FR-Net-EN/fig1-framework.png)

*Figure 1. FR-Net policy training, mass-contact prediction, and cross-platform deployment framework.*

FR-Net consists of an actor, a privileged critic, and MCP. MCP uses five frames of proprioception to estimate four segment-mass values, 13 collision probabilities, and a 16-dimensional latent state. It is trained with next-observation reconstruction, mass estimation, collision classification, and VAE losses.

The actor combines these estimates with current proprioception and outputs 12 joint-position increments. The critic additionally receives height maps, true masses, control gains, center of mass, contact force, and friction. PPO training randomizes morphology, control parameters, payload, center of mass, friction, and terrain difficulty. Rewards constrain posture, contact, force, displacement, joint speed, torque, and action smoothness.

## Experimental setup

![Real-robot recovery on stairs and suspended beams.](/reviews/2026-07-22-FR-Net-EN/fig3-real-world-recovery.png)

*Figure 2. Real-robot recovery on stairs and suspended beams.*

Each method is evaluated with four seeds over ten difficulty levels of rough terrain, stairs, slopes, discrete stones, gaps, and beams. Each level uses 2,048 randomized robots. Success requires stable upright recovery within five seconds and less than 1 m displacement.

Baselines include DreamRiser, RMA, vanilla PPO, and ablations without mass, contact, or full estimation. The real Go2 controller runs at 50 Hz.

## Key results

![Success-rate curves for recovery methods across six challenging terrain families.](/reviews/2026-07-22-FR-Net-EN/fig2-success-rate-results.png)

*Figure 3. Success-rate curves for recovery methods across six challenging terrain families.*

At maximum difficulty, FR-Net reaches `69.5%` success on rough terrain and `64.1%` on beams, exceeding vanilla PPO by `21.8` and `25.3` percentage points. Removing state estimation reduces beam performance by `12.7` points; removing mass prediction reduces gap success from `46.8%` to `35.5%`.

Stability rewards prevent dangerous rolling on 40-degree stairs and slopes. Real Go2 tests cover grass, stones, stairs, foam, discrete obstacles, gaps, and suspended beams. A simulated Unitree G1 also learns recovery on irregular terrain.

## Strengths

- The method advances from implicit adaptation to physically meaningful mass and contact prediction.
- Safety is evaluated through displacement and contact behavior, not only success.
- Morphology randomization provides strong cross-embodiment relevance.
- Ablations isolate the value of mass and contact estimates.

## Weaknesses

- Contact is predicted at the body-part level rather than as precise locations.
- Hidden terrain geometry is still not directly observed.
- Hardware evaluation is scenario-rich but lacks large-sample statistics.

## Discussion questions

1. Why does explicit mass estimation help when deployment uses a fixed robot?
2. Can collision probability be extended to contact location and normal force?
3. How should morphology randomization be balanced against policy conservatism?
4. Can MCP also improve locomotion, manipulation, and collision protection?

## Relevance to our lab

FR-Net is relevant to safety control, collision awareness, morphology generalization, and embodied state estimation. Its use of interpretable physical variables is informative for combining model-based safety layers with learning-based robot control.

## Possible follow-up ideas

- Add distributed tactile sensing for precise contact and force prediction.
- Reuse mass-contact estimation across locomotion and manipulation.
- Add uncertainty outputs and risk-sensitive decisions.
- Build a large-sample, multi-platform real recovery benchmark.
